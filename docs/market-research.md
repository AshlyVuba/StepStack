## How to use this document

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[Verified]** | Checked against a source listed in the Source Register (give its ID, for example S01) |
| **[Assumption]** | Something we are taking as true for planning, not yet proven |
| **[Estimate]** | A number we calculated (show the working and inputs) |
| **[Opinion]** | Our own judgement |
| **[To research]** | Empty placeholder |

**How to use the labels in practice:** A source-backed fact is not automatically a conclusion. For example, an unemployment rate can be [Verified], while the statement that this creates an opportunity for StepStack is an [Opinion] or [Assumption] unless separately tested.

**Rules**

1. Never invent statistics, prices, funding figures or sources.
2. Every statistic, competitor price and funding figure needs a direct URL and publication year. For a live pricing page with no year, record "undated" and the date accessed, and do not mark it Verified until the team agrees how to treat it.
3. Do not delete verified information. If it is outdated, mark it "Superseded" and add the newer entry.
4. Use simple, professional language.

## 4. StepStack Pricing and Unit Economics

**Unit economics** means the money earned and spent for each paying learner or seat.

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| B2C | We sell directly to individual learners |
| B2B | We sell to organisations |
| Seat | One learner's paid place |
| MRR / ARR | Recurring revenue per month / per year |
| Variable cost | A cost that grows with each learner |
| Fixed cost | A cost that stays about the same as learner numbers change |
| Contribution margin | Price minus variable cost for one learner |
| Break-even | The number of learners needed to cover fixed costs |
| CAC | Customer acquisition cost: the cost of winning one customer |
| Churn | The share of customers who stop paying in a period |
| LTV | Lifetime value: the margin expected from a customer before they leave |

### 4.1 Formulas

**Before calculating:** Choose one currency for each scenario and keep it consistent. If the proposed B2C price is in US dollars, either calculate revenue in dollars or convert every relevant amount to rand using one recorded exchange rate and date. Do not mix currencies in the same calculation.

For each formula, write down the inputs used. The results are estimates, not verified market facts, unless they are based on actual StepStack sales and costs.

- Monthly revenue = price per month × paying learners or seats
- Annual revenue = monthly revenue × 12 (assumes numbers and price stay the same all year)
- Contribution margin = price − variable cost per learner
- Monthly profit = monthly revenue − total variable costs − fixed costs
- Break-even learners = fixed monthly costs ÷ contribution margin (round up; margin must be above zero)
- Simple LTV = monthly contribution margin ÷ monthly churn rate
- Rand amount = US dollar amount × exchange rate (record the rate, date and source)

## 6. References and Sources

**Purpose:** Keep a traceable record of every source used in the document. Give each source one ID and reuse that ID wherever you make a claim based on it.

**How to fill in the table**
- **ID:** Give each source a unique ID in order, e.g. S01, S02, S03. Never reuse an ID for a different source.
- **Claim or statistic:** State the exact fact this source supports. One source may support more than one claim; list the important claim(s).
- **Source organisation:** Name the publisher, government department, company or research organisation.
- **Publication year:** Enter the year the report/article was published. For a live webpage with no publication date, write “Undated.”
- **Direct URL:** Link to the original report, official page or exact document, not a search results page or a blog repeating the claim.
- **Date accessed:** Record the date you personally checked the page.
- **Reliability and notes:** Explain why the source is suitable, any limitations, the population or region covered, and whether it is an official or independent source.

**Source quality checks:** Prefer original official statistics for unemployment and internet access; company-owned pages for current product features and prices; and reputable independent reporting for funding or market context. Check the date, geography, definitions and sample/population. A source being official does not mean it supports every interpretation you make.

**Reliability guide (starting point, not a guarantee):** High = official statistics, peer-reviewed research, or a company's own page for its current price. Medium = reputable news or industry reports. Low = blogs, forums or unattributed figures (do not use as sole evidence).
